Post-Peel Skincare Guide: Clinical Recovery Protocols

Chemical peeling is a biological skin renewal method that improves pores, dark spots, and aging by regenerating healthy skin.

What is Chemical Peel?

Chemical peeling uses high-concentration acids (Actives) to remove old, stubborn dead cells and stimulate the growth of new, healthy skin cells. It is an optimal solution for pore issues, pigmentation, acne, and aging.

"The results of a Peel treatment depend not only on the acid concentration but are 70% determined by how you care for your skin at home afterward."

Active Ingredients

water_drop

AHA (Alpha Hydroxy Acid)

Water-soluble acids that work on the skin's surface to remove dead skin, brighten the complexion, and smooth fine lines.

opacity

BHA (Beta Hydroxy Acid)

Oil-soluble Salicylic Acid that penetrates deep into pores to clean sebum and prevent inflammation and acne.

auto_awesome

Retinol

A Vitamin A derivative that accelerates cell turnover, fades dark spots, and promotes structural skin regeneration for anti-aging.

biotech

TCA (Trichloroacetic Acid)

An organic acid used for medium to deep peels, highly effective for structural skin remodeling, deep scars, and wrinkle improvement.

Recovery Phases

1

Days 1 - 3: Sensitive

Skin may experience redness and tingling. Focus on maximum protection and hydration to soothe the barrier.

2

Days 4 - 7: Peeling

Natural peeling begins. Use gentle products and high hydration. Never pick at peeling skin to avoid scarring.

3

Days 7 - 14: Renewal

New, healthy skin becomes rosy and smooth. Continue deep hydration to maintain and optimize the results.

Professional Care Tips

  • check_circle

    Gentle Cleansing

    Use mild, non-foaming, sulfate-free cleansers to avoid further drying or irritating the skin.

  • check_circle

    No Exfoliation

    Stop all forms of scrubs or strong acids for at least the first 10 days of recovery.

  • check_circle

    Hydrate & Repair

    Prioritize products with Peptides, Ceramides, Vitamin E, and B5 to strengthen the skin barrier.

  • check_circle

    Sun Protection

    Always use broad-spectrum SPF 50+ sunscreen, even indoors. New skin is highly susceptible to UV damage.
